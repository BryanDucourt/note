## Q0. "Copies or Sources?": which of the earlier numbers are right, and where each comes from

### Takeaway
Both earlier notes are numerically correct and do not actually conflict: they report different quantities from the same paper. 0.06–0.42 (copy weight), 5–40% (belief-implied early commitment), 0.08 or less (declaration) and 11.2% → 1.1% (reference rule) are all in the abstract and main text. 5.6–34.6 points is the effect on emitted commit-or-query actions. It appears only in appendix Table 18; the main text rounds it to 6–35 points, and Tables 15–17 hold belief-implied rates, not these values. "Labels alone barely help" is not the paper's wording. The paper shows that provenance labels lower the copy weight somewhat but leave most of it.

### Cited Findings
- **Copy weight 0.06–0.42 (correct).** On controlled logs (T1), two forwarded copies carrying `forwarded_from` provenance labels raise mean reported evidence from 1 reading to 1.12 (GPT-6.1 Sol), 1.42 (Gemini 3.8 Flash), 1.55 (GPT-6 Luna) and 1.84 (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash). That gives copy weights w3 = 0.06, 0.21, 0.27, 0.42 (§4.1; main [Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S3.T1)). The values with 95% CIs (Luna 0.27 [0.25, 0.30], Sol 0.06 [0.05, 0.07], Gemini 0.21 [0.19, 0.24], DeepSeek 0.42 [0.39, 0.45]) are in [appendix Table 32](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T32). The abstract states the same range, as a fraction of a new reading — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06192).
- **5–40% (correct).** Belief-implied early commitment P(ê > 2) on Copies (one reading stated three times, 1,000 items): Luna 24.6% [22.0, 27.2], Sol 4.7% [3.4, 6.0], Gemini 19.1% [16.7, 21.5], DeepSeek 39.8% [36.8, 42.9]. Single, Pointers and Padding stay at ≤0.3% — [appendix Table 15](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T15); §4.4 and [Figure 3b](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S4.F3). The oracle never commits on these logs because the boundary is set at e_b = 2 readings (§2.3, Eq. 4) — [§2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1).
- **5.6–34.6 points (correct, but only Table 18).** The models are shown the costs and emit COMMIT or QUERY on the 500-item subset. The row labelled "commitment added by copies (points)" (Copies minus Single, paired by item) reads Luna 21.8 [17.8, 25.8], Sol 5.6 [3.2, 8.0], Gemini 24.2 [19.8, 28.8], DeepSeek 34.6 [28.6, 40.4] — [appendix Table 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T18). The main text rounds this to 6–35 percentage points (§4.4) — [§4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1).
- **What Tables 15–17 actually contain.** Tables 15–16 give belief-implied early commitment at e_b = 2 and e_b = 1.15, plus unnecessary queries on Independent. Table 17 compares one reply with the median of three replies. All are belief-implied, not emitted actions — [Tables 15](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T15), [16](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T16), [17](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T17). Appendix tables are numbered separately from the main text: appendix Table 1 lists the episode types ([A1.T1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A1.T1)). "Table 15" therefore means an appendix table; the main text has only Tables 1–3.
- **Belief vs. emitted action agreement.** Emitted added commitment minus belief-implied commitment: Luna −5.6 [−10.4, −1.0], Sol −0.8, Gemini +3.4, DeepSeek −3.2 points. The −5.6 here is Luna's difference, not Sol's 5.6-point effect — [Table 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T18).
- **Raw emitted commitment rates (no note, 500 items).** Single 2.4 / 1.0 / 6.2 / 35.8%; Copies 24.2 / 6.6 / 30.4 / 70.4%; Independent (where committing is correct) 89.4 / 98.8 / 99.2 / 98.4%; Copied minority 8.0 / 3.2 / 21.6 / 53.4%, in the order Luna / Sol / Gemini / DeepSeek. DeepSeek commits on 35.8% of single-reading logs even without copies — [Table 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T18).
- **Declaration → copy weight ≤ 0.08 (correct; 500-item subset).** Under "Declaration: records", ê on Copies is 1.01 / 1.00 / 1.01 / 1.15, against 1.61 / 1.15 / 1.46 / 1.81 with no note (main Table 1, lower part). Two further wordings give w3 ≤ 0.09 (§5.1) — [Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S3.T1), [§5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1).
- **Reference rule 11.2% → 1.1% (correct; agent-written logs, T3).** This is early commitment on one-observer logs, pooled over four protocols and averaged over the four aggregator models (§5.2). By protocol: relay 12.1 → 0.4, board 13.6 → 0.4, discussion 13.2 → 0.3, hierarchy 6.1 → 3.3 — [Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S4.T3).
- **Labels.** With labels, Copies gives 1.12–1.84 readings. Dropping the labels raises this to 1.20–1.97 (§4.2; [Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S3.T1)), and belief-implied early commitment rises from 4.7–39.8% to 8.5–46.1% ("Copies (no labels)", [Table 15](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T15)). A field guide explaining what `forwarded_from` means leaves the copy weight at 0.04–0.37, against 0.08–0.40 without it (§5.1; [Table 21](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T21)).

### Inferences
- Earlier note A attached appendix Table 18's emitted-action numbers to "Tables 15–18". The correct citation for 5.6–34.6 is appendix Table 18 alone. For 4.7–39.8% it is appendix Table 15 (or §4.4).
- The two notes describe complementary channels: belief (calibration in readings) and emitted action. The paper shows they agree to within about 6 points at its one tested cost, so the substantive claim that copies cause commit-instead-of-query holds on both channels.
- "Labels barely help" should be replaced by: correct provenance labels reduce but do not remove copy counting. Explaining the labels does almost nothing. Only a declaration that copies add no evidence (or explicit extraction of the distinct readings) removes most of it.

### Gaps
- None for these numbers; each one was located in the full text. Main Figure 3 (curves over m and e_b) exists only as a chart, so values beyond those given in the text and Tables 15–16 were not read off it.

## Q2. Setting: text-only or embodied, single or multi-agent, which models and how many

### Takeaway
None of the five is embodied or uses images. [CS] is the closest in setting: an LLM aggregator reads multi-agent message logs framed as sensor readings, including rover and monitoring domains. It uses four API models from three providers. [GE] is a single text graph-walking agent (four models). [EC] is a multi-agent deliberation controller over one backbone. [ES] uses one small model only as an extractor. [WF] is single-turn multiple-choice QA over 13 open-weight models.

### Cited Findings
- **[CS] setting.** Text only. An LLM aggregator reads a message log and reports a probability. The three testbeds are T1, controlled JSON or prose logs of sensor readings with an exact Bayesian oracle; T2, web documents from ConflictingQA; and T3, logs written by teams of four LLM agents under relay, board, discussion and hierarchy protocols (§3) — [HTML §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1), [Figure 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S1.F2). The T1 domains include a planetary-rover surface-operations team (dust storm), reservoir ecology, a chemical-plant coolant loop, city air quality and a coastal algal bloom ([appendix Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A2.T3)), but all evidence is text records, not perception.
- **[CS] models.** Four models from three providers: GPT-6 Luna, GPT-6.1 Sol, Gemini 3.8 Flash and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, called through official APIs at low reasoning effort with no sampling parameters set (§3 "Models and analysis"; [appendix Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A1.T2)). T3 worker agents are GPT-6 Luna with reasoning off. A second worker model (DeepSeek, thinking off) writes 800 more logs. Annotation is done by Sol at high effort, which matches human labels on 98.8% of 240 messages (§3 T3; Appendix D.2) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1).
- **[GE] setting.** A text-only LLM graph agent, single agent. The agent sees the current node, visited nodes, neighbors with short previews and the remaining walk budget, and chooses walk or stop (§4.1). Data: GraphEcho-Syn has 800 fictional claims in biomedicine, materials, agriculture and economics (500 train / 100 dev / 200 test). SciFact (dev split) gives 188 claims and 584 graph instances (§3.2, §3.4) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1).
- **[GE] models.** Frozen Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B (greedy decoding; 1.7B and 8B in non-thinking mode), plus GPT-5-mini as a supplementary API baseline, so four frozen agents. PAPT post-trains Qwen3-4B (§5.1, §4.3) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1).
- **[EC] setting.** Text-only multi-agent deliberation (proposer, challenger, verifier, arbitrator roles) on MedQA, CaseHOLD and FinQA. A trained controller chooses propose / challenge / verify / arbitrate / diversify / correct / stop over frozen role-conditioned LLMs (§3) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17869v1). Backbone: gpt-5.4 for every method in the main evaluation. A weak-backbone diagnostic uses gpt-4-turbo on 18 records (Appendix B.1, Table 6; [Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17869v1#A2.T8)).
- **[ES] setting.** Theory plus a text-only empirical study. A single small Haiku-class language model reads synthetic memos about a fictional company's quarterly revenue and extracts a revenue estimate. Aggregation is done by statistical aggregators, not by an LLM: a naive independent-Gaussian aggregator, a provenance-aware shared-root aggregator, a correlated-extraction aggregator, and an embedding-plus-cosine-clustering deduplication baseline (§8.1) — [HTML §8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01873v1). The study makes more than 20,000 LLM calls ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01873)). Reproducibility is stated as not requiring the Anthropic API, and a larger alternative model was rejected because it exposes no sampling temperature (§8.6) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01873v1).
- **[WF] setting.** Text-only, single-turn, forced-choice multiple-choice QA. Two or three Markdown tables of fictional NeoQA entities carry conflicting attribute values and are attributed to synthetic sources: government, newspaper, social media user or person. The answer is read from deterministic A/B token probabilities, not generations (§3.1–3.3) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3). Models: 13 instruction-tuned open-weight models, namely Qwen2.5 7B/14B/32B/72B, OLMo-2 7B/13B/32B, Llama-3.2 3B, Llama-3.1 8B/70B and Gemma-3 4B/12B/27B (§3.3) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3).

### Inferences
- In [ES], the "overconfidence" result belongs to a formal aggregator that assumes independence. It says nothing directly about whether an LLM planner's own belief inflates with copies. That is the question Paper 1, [CS], [GE] and [WF] ask.
- No paper has a planner that acts in an environment, observes images, or writes to typed memory stores.

### Gaps
- [ES] does not name the exact model beyond "Haiku-class" in the sections read (§1, §7–§11). The supplementary experimental log it mentions was not checked.

## Q3. What is varied (number of copies, provenance labels, independent vs. same-source evidence, paths, context length)

### Takeaway
[CS] varies nearly every factor Paper 1 plans to vary, in text: copy count (m = 1, 2, 3, 5, 8), provenance labels on or off, format (JSON or prose), token-length-matched padding, pointers instead of restatements, and copies against independent sources. It also varies the agent protocol that creates the copies. [GE] varies path count against origin count with content fixed. [ES] varies report count against evidence-root count, and representation against ancestry. [WF] varies source type and repetition (same source twice, two sources, merged into one table). [EC] runs no controlled duplication manipulation.

### Cited Findings
- **[CS] T1 forms.** 1,000 items (5 domains × 5 reliabilities q ∈ {0.60, …, 0.80} × 2 polarities × 20), each rendered in 27 forms ([appendix Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A2.T4); §3 T1). The forms are: Single; Copies_m for m ∈ {2, 3, 5, 8} (forwards from relays); Copies (one relay); Pointers (records whose value is "see r1"); Padding_m (heartbeat records token-matched to Copies or Copies8); Packed (one record whose relay trace states the value 3 or 8 times); Claims (equality claims without the value); Independent_m; Copies with no labels, in prose, and in prose without labels; Independent variants; Tie; Copied tie (e* = 0); Copied minority (e* = −1) — [Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A2.T4).
- **[CS] aggregator notes and reasoning** (500-item subset): no note, counting instruction, declarations (records, relays, copies), field guide, three placebos (unrelated, delivery, display), and reasoning effort off / low / high — [Table 1 lower part](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S3.T1), [appendix Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A1.T1).
- **[CS] T2.** 500 ConflictingQA items. Two items are appended to the base [F, O]: exact duplicates of F, republished copies naming F's outlet, LLM summaries attributed to F, or two distinct documents from other domains. Each condition runs with and without a declaration (10 conditions) — [Appendix C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1), §3 T2.
- **[CS] T3.** 500 configurations × 4 protocols × {one observer (e* = 1), three observers (e* = 3)} × {no rule, reference rule}, giving 4,000 + 4,000 logs. Aggregator arms: no note, declaration, extract-first, true list (§3 T3; Appendix D.1; [appendix Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A4.T5)).
- **[CS] pairing.** Every model answers every condition on the same items, so each contrast differs in one factor (§3) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1).
- **[GE].** Structural multiplicity N_path and provenance multiplicity N_prov are varied separately (conditions sSkP, Eq. 1–2). The primary contrasts are 1S1P vs 1S5P (redundant paths) and 1S5P vs 5S5P (distinct origins at fixed path count), with k = 3 as an extra condition. Evidence wording and relation templates are identical between 1S5P and 5S5P (§3.1–3.2, §5.2) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1). The conflict conditions C1, C2 (support repeated over 5 routes), C3 (5 distinct supporting origins), C4 and C2R are defined in [Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1#S3.T1). Provenance visibility is varied by mitigation: M0 shows source nodes only, M1 adds provenance identifiers, M2 adds an instruction to count origins, M3 collapses equivalent paths (§4.2). Walk budgets: 6 (primary), plus 3, 5 and 8 (§4.1).
- **[GE] context length.** Context length is not held fixed in the redundant-path contrast, since more paths means more text. Token counts are kept only as a diagnostic (§3.2) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1).
- **[ES].** Grid A holds one evidence root and varies report count n ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32} (300 evaluation worlds). Grid B holds n = 16 and varies root count k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}. A 2×2 design crosses shared vs. independent roots with two rationale-rendering styles, the rendering being applied after elicitation so the estimates stay unchanged (200 worlds) (§8.2, §8.3, §8.5) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01873v1).
- **[WF].** Source type (government, newspaper, social media, person, or none) and within-type features (popularity, regionality, gender, age, academic titles, username style) (§4.1–4.2). Majority vs. repetition is tested in three layouts: *2-Table Majority* (two identical tables from two different social-media users vs. one government table), *1-Table Majority* (the two users merged into one table header), and *Repetition* (two identical tables from the same user). Repetition is also tested with unattributed tables (§5; example prompts in [appendix Figures 27–28](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3#A8.F28)). Every prompt is presented in both orders to control position bias (§3.3) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3).
- **[EC].** No manipulation of duplication. The independent variable is the controller: state-conditioned vs. fixed schedule, shuffled states, no risk head, no role memory, compared against seven baselines (§5.1, [Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17869v1#S5.T2)).

### Inferences
- Paper 1's planned manipulations of copy count, provenance labels and context length have already been run in text by [CS], with a padding control matched to copy token length. What remains new for Paper 1 is how the copies are produced: one LLM write fanned out into several typed memory stores. Holding images fixed and carrying the copied content as a misperception are also new.
- [WF]'s 1-Table vs 2-Table contrast is a form-of-presentation manipulation, like [CS]'s Pointers vs. Copies. Both suggest that writing the agreement once, rather than restating it, is what matters.

### Gaps
- [CS] does not vary copies that disagree slightly with the original, such as relays that alter content; §7 lists these as future work — [HTML §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1).

## Q4. What is measured (beliefs, calibration, answers, actions, verification/query choices, costs swept or fixed, error persistence over steps)

### Takeaway
[CS] measures calibrated belief in units of independent readings against an exact oracle, belief-implied commit-or-query decisions across a sweep of cost boundaries, and emitted commit-or-query actions at one cost. [GE] measures stated confidence and verdicts plus active acquisition: repeated walks and source recall under fixed budgets. [ES] measures coverage and calibration of statistical aggregators. [WF] measures shifts in answer probability. [EC] measures accuracy, false consensus and evidence support. None measures error persistence or spread across steps of an episode.

### Cited Findings
- **[CS] belief.** The reported probability is converted to evidence in readings, ê = a·logit(p̂)/L. The copy weight is w_m = (ē − 1)/(m − 1) and the discrimination is D_m (Eqs. 2–3, §2.2). The source-counting oracle is exact for T1 and T3 (Eq. 1) — [HTML §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1).
- **[CS] decisions.** An aggregator commits (+1 or −1) or pays c for one more reading of reliability r. The commit boundary in readings is e_b = logit(r − c/2)/L (Eq. 4), and the paper notes that changing the cost structure only shifts e_b (§2.3). Belief-implied early commitment is reported at e_b = 2 and e_b = 1.15 and as a curve over e_b ∈ [1, 3] (Appendix E; [Figure 3c–d](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S4.F3)). Emitted actions are measured with the costs shown, at one setting: c = 0.05, with r set per q so that e_b = 2 (§3 T1; [Table 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T18)). Unnecessary queries on Independent are also reported ([Table 16](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T16)).
- **[CS] other measures.** Consistency across three replies to the same log ([Table 17](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T17)); belief-stage vs. action-stage errors ([Table 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T18)); T3 restatement counts μ from annotated transcripts ([Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S4.T3)). Each decision is single-shot per log: there is no multi-step episode in which an error could persist (§2.3, §3) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1).
- **[CS] statistics.** Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on ê, exact McNemar tests on actions and Page's trend test, with Holm correction within families fixed in advance. Null controls must fall within ±0.10 readings (§3; [appendix Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A5.T6)).
- **[GE].** Static measures: confidence (subjective claim probability), PME_k and ISE_k (Eq. 4–5), evidence-count inflation, topology flip rate and conflict bias (§5.2). Acquisition measures: unique provenance coverage UPC = U/V, echo walk rate EWR = E/W, unique source recall USR = U/D, walk efficiency WE = U/W, and source-count error (§5.3). The limitations state that self-reported probabilities do not establish calibration. Action cost enters only as a fixed reward term, −0.05·W/B (§4.3) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1).
- **[ES].** 95% interval coverage, calibration ratio C = RMSE / posterior s.d., mean negative log score, block-mean variance (to estimate extraction-error correlation), and dedup cluster counts with false-merge and false-split rates (§8.2–8.5) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01873v1). No decisions, verification or costs.
- **[WF].** Source preference SP = P(A | attributed context) − P(A | unattributed context), averaged over pairs (§3.3); the "SP gap" with and without repetition or majority (§5); the probability of a third, abstain option, in §4.4 only, without repetition ([Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3#S4.T1)) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3).
- **[EC].** Accuracy; false-consensus rate (FCR: confident convergence that is incorrect or unsupported); evidence-support rate (ESR), both judged by frozen LLM judges with sampled human audits (judge–human agreement 0.81–0.86, ECE 0.03–0.05); token use and rounds; action-routing shares (§5.1; [appendix Table 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17869v1#A2.T7)).

### Inferences
- Paper 1's planned "verification choices across a sweep of verification costs" is half-anticipated. [CS] sweeps the boundary only for belief-implied decisions and tests emitted actions at a single cost. An emitted-action cost sweep, with real re-observation actions, is still open.
- "Error persistence over steps and spread to progress judgments and next actions" is measured by none of the five. This looks like Paper 1's clearest distinguishing outcome.

### Gaps
- [GE]'s per-condition acquisition curves (appendix Figure 4) were seen only as captions.

## Q5. Main quantitative results (with table, figure or section)

### Takeaway
[CS]: every model counts a forwarded copy as a fraction of a reading (0.06–0.42). This arises mostly from occasional replies that count every copy fully, and it tips 5–40% of single-reading logs past the commit boundary. [GE]: redundant paths raise repeated walks in all four agents (+5.8 to +38.8 points), while the direction of the confidence change depends on the model. [ES]: naive coverage collapses from 0.940 to 0.263 as reports multiply over one root, and the same model's replicate extraction errors correlate (γ ≈ 0.72). ### Inferences
- [CS]'s finding that copy counting is mostly all-or-nothing implies that mean calibration can look acceptable while a sizeable minority of decisions flip. Paper 1 should report per-decision crossing rates and the variability of replies, not only mean confidence.
- [GE] shows the sign of the confidence response to redundancy differs across models (Qwen3-4B up; Qwen3-8B and GPT-5-mini down). This supports Paper 1's plan to use at least two planner LLMs. It also warns that "duplicates cause overconfidence" may not hold for every planner.
- [GE]'s acquisition result points in a different direction from Paper 1's hypothesis. Redundancy there produced more repeated visits to the same origin, not fewer checks. Paper 1 should separate re-checking the same source from acquiring independent evidence.
- [ES]'s γ ≈ 0.72 suggests that re-reading the same input with the same model is far from an independent check. Paper 1's "verify" action should probably be a genuinely new observation, such as a new view or image, rather than re-querying the same image.
- [EC]'s headline numbers rest on aggregate records that the authors state cannot yet be re-aggregated. They should be treated as weak evidence.

- [WF]'s per-model repetition values appear only in figures; only the averages (33.90, 6.17, 30.04) were extracted from the text.

## Q6. Remedies proposed, and how well they work

### Takeaway
[CS] has the most directly reusable remedies. A one-paragraph declaration that copies add no evidence brings w3 to ≤0.08. Extracting the distinct readings first removes early commitment on agent logs. A protocol-side reference rule ("refer, don't restate") cuts early commitment from 11.2% to 1.1%. All three preserve genuine corroboration. [GE] finds that provenance IDs and instructions do not help, while collapsing paths or provenance-aware RL do, at a cost in coverage. [ES] uses a provenance-aware aggregator, refined with a correlated-extraction model. [WF] uses prompting (partial) and distillation fine-tuning (−79.2% repetition bias). [EC]'s controller gates stopping on an independence-aware false-consensus risk.

### Cited Findings
- **[CS] declaration.** w3 ≤ 0.08 on controlled logs. The response to independent readings moves by ≤0.06 readings, and siding with the copied minority falls to ≤3%. A counting instruction alone gives w3 = 0.01–0.18, and ≤0.03 when added to the declaration. Same-length placebos change w by ≤0.03, but a placebo that draws attention to how forwarded records are displayed *raises* ê by 0.27–0.39 for three models (§5.1; [Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S3.T1)). For emitted actions, the declaration brings the extra commitment caused by copies to ≤1 point for three models and 11 points for DeepSeek (Copies 45.6% vs. Single 35.0%) (§5.3; [Table 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T18)). On web copies it reaches less far: ρ falls to 0.07–0.18 for duplicates and 0.09–0.30 for republished copies, while summaries keep 0.19–0.47 (§5.1; [Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S4.T2)).
- **[CS] reasoning effort.** High effort lowers w3 to 0.01 (Sol) – 0.20 (DeepSeek); reasoning off raises it to 0.40 (Luna) and 0.55 (DeepSeek) (§5.1; [appendix Table 23](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T23)).
- **[CS] extract-first** (a second model call). On T3 one-observer logs the aggregators then report 0.95–0.99 readings, with no early commitment in any of the 2,000 one-observer logs per model. Its list matches the true readings in 93–97% of logs (§5.1, §5.3; [Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S4.T2)).
- **[CS] reference rule** (in the agents' prompts). Restating messages fall from 71% to 10%. In relay, board and discussion the median μ drops to 1, the aggregators report 0.95–1.03 readings despite copy weights that differ sevenfold, and mean early commitment falls from 12–14% to ≤0.5% (DeepSeek: 23–25% → ≤1.4%). The hierarchy still restates (median 2, 33% of messages). Pooled early commitment goes from 11.2% to 1.1% (§5.2; [Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S4.T3)).
- **[CS] corroboration preserved.** With three observers, every remedy stays within 0.06–0.07 readings of the true-list value (2.90–2.96), or within 0.02 under the declaration for three models (§5.3; [Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S4.T2)).
- **[GE] cheap controls** (Qwen3-4B, seed 0). M1 (provenance IDs) and M2 (instruction) leave accuracy at 42.50% and 42.12% and echo rates at 41.97% and 41.72%, close to M0 (43.12%, 40.52%). M3 (collapse) reaches 83.25% accuracy with 0.00% echo and 95.88% recall. PAPT reaches 87.12% accuracy, 4.17% echo and 80.48% recall ([Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1#S6.T4)). Against M1 over five seeds, PAPT gains +41.47 pp synthetic accuracy and cuts echo by −36.89 pp, but source recall drops −8.32 pp, SciFact accuracy −3.55 pp, and SciFact echo falls by 36.00 pp ([Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1#S6.T5)). PAPT averages about two walks and reaches about one-third of the six available origins in C3 (§6.3). Statically, M3 nearly eliminates PME (0.05) while keeping ISE (5.90); PAPT keeps PME at 7.35 ([appendix Table 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1#A6.T7)).
- **[ES].** The provenance-aware aggregator keeps coverage within 0.850–0.940 in Grid A (§8.2). The exploratory correlated-extraction aggregator restores coverage to 0.940–0.953, with calibration ratio 0.95–0.96 (§8.4). Report-space deduplication by embedding similarity fails to track ancestry (§8.5). The theory says report content alone cannot identify ancestry, so provenance (side information) plus a dependence model are both needed (§9) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01873v1).
- **[WF] prompting.** A repetition-aware instruction (identify the supporting sources, assess their credibility, ignore simple repetition) weakens repetition bias. It does not restore the original hierarchy, except for the largest Llama model (§5; [Figure 10](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3#S5.F10); Appendix I).
- **[WF] fine-tuning.** Teacher-student KL distillation on Gemma-3-4B with LoRA (r = 8, α = 16), trained on newspaper and person sources and tested on unseen government and social-media sources, combined with a credibility prompt. The government vs. no-source SP gap falls by 79.2% (45.9 → 9.55) while 72.5% of the original preference is retained (29.6 → 21.5). For government vs. social media the gap falls by 78.3% (33.2 → 7.2). Results are averaged over 7 seeds (§6; [Figure 11](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3#S6.F11); Appendix K). One internal inconsistency: λ is 0.2 in §6 but 200 in Appendix K (v3 HTML) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3).
- **[EC] controller.** The false-consensus risk is r_fc = g·(1 − H_sup)·(1 − I_ind). A calibrated risk head vetoes stopping, so the policy can challenge, verify or diversify (§3.2) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17869v1). Evidence independence I_ind is the mean pairwise dissimilarity among evidence embeddings after deduplication ([appendix Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17869v1#A1.T4)). Results are in Q5.

### Inferences
- [CS]'s declaration and reference rule are cheap and strong text-level baselines, and Paper 1 should include them as remedy arms. A shared-memory analogue of the reference rule would be: write the observation once to one store, and let other stores hold pointers to it.
- [EC] measures independence by embedding dissimilarity, the kind of report-space signal that [ES] §8.5 shows can follow wording rather than ancestry. Paper 1 can cite this pair to argue for explicit provenance in memory writes.
- [GE]'s PAPT shows a remedy that cuts repetition can also shorten acquisition and leave evidence unseen. Paper 1 should check that any "suppress duplicates" remedy does not also suppress useful verification.

### Gaps
- None of the remedies has been tested in an embodied agent, or with memory stores written by one LLM call.

## Q7. Overlap with Paper 1 (high/medium/low) and what Paper 1 would still add

### Takeaway
Overlap is **high with [CS]**. In text settings it already establishes that same-source copies inflate LLM belief and push commit-instead-of-query decisions, with copy-count dose, provenance-label and padding controls, a cost-boundary sweep, four models, and declaration and protocol remedies. Overlap is **medium with [GE]** (redundancy vs. provenance with active acquisition), **low–medium with [ES] and [WF]**, and **low with [EC]**. What Paper 1 still adds: an embodied setting with images held fixed; duplication created by one LLM write fanned out into typed memory stores, with a misperception as the copied content; emitted verification (re-observation) choices across a sweep of verification costs; and error persistence and spread to progress judgments and next actions over multi-step episodes. None of the five measures persistence or spread.

### Cited Findings
- **[CS] (High).** Same mechanism and outcome (copies → inflated belief → commit instead of query) — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06192). It already covers copy count m ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 8}, provenance labels on or off, token-matched padding and pointers ([appendix Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A2.T4)); a boundary sweep equivalent to any cost structure (§2.3; [Figure 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#S4.F3)); emitted actions at one cost ([Table 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1#A6.T18)); four models; and a board protocol, i.e. a shared blackboard (§3 T3). Its introduction names blackboards and shared memories among the places where restatements arise (§1) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1). Its own limits: honest, exact copies with correct labels, a fully visible log, and single-shot decisions. Forged identifiers, relays that alter content, and sources with shared failure modes are left open (§7) — [HTML §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1).
- **[GE] (Medium).** Text graph agent; path count vs. origin count with content fixed. It measures judgments and active acquisition under walk budgets of 3–8, and tests provenance IDs (no help) and training (helps at a coverage cost) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1). It is not embodied, has no oracle-based calibration, and uses no cost sweep beyond budgets (PAPT vs. frozen only, [appendix Table 11](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17695v1#A6.T11)).
- **[ES] (Low–Medium).** It supplies theory (report-only non-identifiability; shared-root ceiling; correlated extraction) and shows an independence-assuming statistical aggregator becoming miscalibrated as reports multiply. It has no LLM aggregator or planner, no actions, no verification and no steps — [HTML §8](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01873v1).
- **[WF] (Low–Medium).** Repetition of the same source flips LLM answers in single-turn text MCQA across 13 models, with prompting and fine-tuning remedies. It has no calibration against an oracle, no actions, verification or costs, and no multi-step dynamics (§5–6) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3).
- **[EC] (Low).** A controller treats confident agreement that rests on duplicated or correlated support as false-consensus risk and can trigger verification, but it never manipulates duplication. The setting is QA with one backbone, and the main results are aggregate-only — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17869); Appendix B.1 of [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17869v1).
- **Related lead: Lin et al. 2026** (cited by [CS] §6), "Beyond Memory Majority: Latent-Source Reasoning for Multi-Agent Memory Arbitration", arXiv 2608.19701 (2026-08-20). Per its abstract, memories written by different agents may inherit the same upstream source and form a false majority, termed Memory Correlation Bias. Its CAMA method estimates the effective number of independent sources and learns a sequential recovery policy that retrieves alternative evidence or traces upstream sources while minimizing retrieval cost — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19701). Only the abstract was checked.
- **Related lead: Liao 2026** (cited by [CS] §6), "Auditing Provenance Sensitivity in LLM Agent Action Selection", arXiv 2607.20827. Per its abstract, it varies the source authority of a proposition on 450 controlled next-action tasks. Trusted and untrusted variants change actions in 5.4% of competing cases vs. 1.7% of supporting cases — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20827). Only the abstract was checked.
- **Other related titles in [CS]'s reference list** (not checked): GullibleBench, "A Causal Benchmark for Manufactured Consensus" (GitHub, 2026); Dai 2026, "When Consensus Is Not Correctness: Diversity Collapse and Manufactured Overconfidence in Multi-Agent LLM Debate" (KDD workshop); Wu 2026, "Split the Labor: Separating Evidence Interpretation from Decision Aggregation" (arXiv 2608.14509); Naphade 2026, "Rational Synthesizers or Heuristic Followers?" (Findings of ACL 2026); Kohli 2026, "Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes" (arXiv 2605.29800); Cho & Lee 2026, "RARE" (ACL 2026) — [CS references](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1).

### Inferences
- Paper 1 can no longer claim the basic text-level effect as new: same-source copies inflate confidence and reduce querying, and provenance labels only partly help. [CS] owns that claim with a cleaner oracle than an embodied task can offer. Paper 1 should cite [CS] as the text-level baseline and present itself as testing whether, and how, the effect carries over to, and compounds in, embodied agents with shared memory.
- The strongest remaining contributions, from the five papers read:
  1. The write-side fan-out mechanism: one LLM call writing one misperception into task, object, spatial and experience stores, which differs from [CS]'s relay, board, discussion and hierarchy protocols.
  2. Misperception as the copied content, so that copies of a wrong reading interact with the image evidence held fixed.
  3. Emitted verification choices over a sweep of real verification costs, which [CS] does only belief-implied.
  4. Error persistence over steps and spread to progress judgments and next actions, which none of the five measure.
  5. Remedy transfer: whether [CS]'s declaration and reference rule work when copies sit in typed memory stores, and whether a "pointer instead of copy" memory write removes the effect.
- Before any novelty claim, Lin et al. 2026 (2608.19701) needs a full-text check. Its abstract covers shared multi-agent memory, same-upstream-source false majorities, and an active verification (recovery) policy with retrieval cost, which may be closer to Paper 1's memory framing than [EC], [ES] or [WF].
- Paper 1 should pre-register a handling for [GE]'s model-dependent sign. If a planner reacts to duplicates by lowering confidence or over-checking, that is a different failure from overconfidence and should be reported as such.
