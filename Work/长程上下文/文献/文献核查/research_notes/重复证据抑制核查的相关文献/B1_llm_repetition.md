# B1: Repeated, majority and duplicated evidence in LLM context: effects on answers, confidence, calibration and verification; correlated errors; hallucination snowballing

Scope note: these notes are the novelty check for Paper 1's claim. In an LLM embodied agent with shared memory, one misperception copied into several memory stores and shown as if it came from several sources makes the planner overconfident and less likely to verify, so the error persists and spreads. Search date: 2026-10-21. I ran arXiv API keyword searches (about 60 queries), batch-fetched about 75 abstracts, and read the full text of 4 key papers (Schuster et al. 2026, Xie et al. 2024, Jin et al. 2024, Xu et al. 2024). Overlap ratings (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW) are relative to the Paper 1 claim. "Venue not re-checked" means the arXiv record did not list a venue and I am giving it from memory.

## Key Question 1: Illusory-truth and repetition effects in LLMs (in context and in training): does repetition raise acceptance or confidence?

### Takeaway
Yes, but the effect depends on the model. Several studies show that repeating a claim in context makes LLMs accept it more often and lowers their uncertainty about it (Fastowski & Kasneci 2024: uncertainty falls by up to 52.8% after repeated exposure). Repetition effects in LLM "participants" range from a true illusory-truth effect to none, or even skepticism (Bouleimen et al., COLM 2026). Only one study separates same-source duplication from distinct sources (Schuster et al. 2026, see KQ2). None measures calibration (whether confidence matches accuracy) or any downstream verify, observe or act decision.

### Cited Findings
- **[Overlap: HIGH–MEDIUM] Understanding Knowledge Drift in LLMs through Misinformation.** Alina Fastowski, Gjergji Kasneci (2024). DELTA workshop @ KDD 2024. arXiv:2409.07085. *Method:* false information inserted into QA prompts; factuality and uncertainty measured with entropy, perplexity and token probability. *Finding:* uncertainty rises by up to 56.6% when the model answers wrongly after one exposure. Repeated exposure to the same false information then lowers uncertainty again, by 52.8% relative to answers on untainted prompts, which the authors read as manipulation of the model's beliefs ("knowledge drift"). *Difference:* the closest direct evidence that repetition inflates confidence. However, it is single-turn QA with no provenance or source-independence control, no calibration metric (ECE or Brier), and no verification or action. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07085)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] Illusory Truth or Mere Exposure? Model-Dependent Repetition Effects in LLM-Based Social Media Simulations.** Azza Bouleimen, Nicolò Pagan, Anikó Hannák (2026). COLM 2026. arXiv:2609.36278. *Method:* two-phase within-context design. Statements are repeated in a simulated news feed and then rated. 336,000 truth, importance, sentiment and interest ratings from 4 LLMs, analyzed with OLS and mixed-effects models. *Finding:* "four qualitatively distinct patterns". Gemma-3-4B shows a genuine illusory-truth effect, Qwen2.5-7B a mere-exposure effect, GPT-5-nano no truth boost and mild skepticism toward repeated content, and Llama-3.1-8B a small truth boost. Temperature has no effect, and ratings are highly sensitive to context. *Difference:* the outcome is truth ratings in a social simulation. There is no decision, verification or provenance manipulation. Its main lesson for Paper 1 is that results will vary across models, which supports using two or more planners. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36278)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–LOW] Susceptibility to Influence of Large Language Models.** Lewis D. Griffin, Bennett Kleinberg, Maximilian Mozes, et al. (2023). arXiv preprint. arXiv:2303.06074. *Method:* 1,000 LLM-simulated participants and 1,000 humans go through an illusory-truth paradigm (an exposure task, then truth ratings). *Finding:* simulated participants reproduce the human pattern. Prior exposure raises later truth ratings, and only for the truth attribute. *Difference:* the LLM acts as a model of human cognition. There is no agent, no source structure and no confidence or verification measure. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06074)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] The Earth is Flat because...: Investigating LLMs' Belief towards Misinformation via Persuasive Conversation (Farm dataset).** Rongwu Xu, Brian S. Lin, Shujian Yang, et al. (2024). ACL 2024. arXiv:2312.09085. *Method:* multi-turn persuasion on facts the model knows. Strategies include a plain "repetition" baseline that re-states the false claim over turns with no new argument, plus logical, credibility and emotional appeals. *Finding (full text):* "repetition is more effective than single-turn". The misinformed rate after 4 turns divided by the rate after 1 turn (MR@4/MR@1) is 1.44–1.68 for ChatGPT and 1.42–2.00 for GPT-4. In absolute terms GPT-4 is "nearly immune to repetition" (NQ1 MR@4: repetition 13.7 vs. appeals 45.2–50.2). ChatGPT is about equally swayed by repetition and appeals (64.4 vs. 59.8–66.8), and Llama-2-7B-chat reaches 81.5 under repetition. A token-probability confidence probe (Llama-2-7B, Vicuna-7B) found that confidence in the correct answer dropped after one misinformation turn. *Difference:* the repetition comes from a user in dialogue, not from co-equal memory entries. There is no source-independence manipulation and no verification behavior. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09085v5)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Learning to Break the Loop: Analyzing and Mitigating Repetitions for Neural Text Generation.** Jin Xu, Xiaojiang Liu, Jianhao Yan, et al. (2022). NeurIPS 2022. arXiv:2206.02369. *Finding:* a "self-reinforcement effect": "the more times a sentence is repeated in the context, the higher the probability of continuing to generate that sentence". The effect is stronger for sentences that already had high probability. *Difference:* this is a token-level generation mechanism (GPT-2/BART), not belief or decision. It is a plausible low-level mechanism by which copies in context raise probability-based confidence. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02369)
- **[Overlap: LOW] Calibrate Before Use: Improving Few-Shot Performance of Language Models.** Tony Z. Zhao, Eric Wallace, Shi Feng, Dan Klein, Sameer Singh (2021). ICML 2021. arXiv:2102.09690. *Finding (abstract):* few-shot instability "arises from the bias of language models towards predicting certain answers, e.g., those that are placed near the end of the prompt or are common in the pre-training data". Contextual calibration improves accuracy by up to 30.0 points absolute. The paper body also describes a "majority label bias" toward labels that are frequent among the in-context examples; I did not re-check this in the full text this session. *Difference:* label frequency among in-context examples, not duplicated factual evidence. It is still a canonical citation that answer frequency in context shifts outputs. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09690)
- **[Overlap: LOW] Characterizing Mechanisms for Factual Recall in Language Models.** Qinan Yu, Jack Merullo, Ellie Pavlick (2023). EMNLP 2023 (venue not re-checked). arXiv:2310.15910. *Finding:* in Pythia and GPT-2, how often the query country and the in-context city appear in training strongly affects whether a counterfactual prefix overrides memorized knowledge. Scaling a single attention head pushes in-context answer use to 88%. *Difference:* training frequency and mechanism, not in-context copy count. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15910)
- **[Overlap: LOW] Training-side frequency and duplication (background only).**
  - Kandpal, Deng, Roberts, Wallace, Raffel (2023), "Large Language Models Struggle to Learn Long-Tail Knowledge", ICML 2023, arXiv:2211.08411. QA accuracy shows "strong correlational and causal relationships" with the number of relevant pretraining documents. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08411)
  - Carlini, Ippolito, Jagielski, Lee, Tramèr, Zhang (2022/23), "Quantifying Memorization Across Neural Language Models", ICLR 2023 (venue not re-checked), arXiv:2202.07646. Memorization grows log-linearly with "the number of times an example has been duplicated". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07646)
  - Kalai & Vempala (2024), "Calibrated Language Models Must Hallucinate", STOC 2024, arXiv:2311.14648. For calibrated LMs, the hallucination rate on arbitrary facts is close to "the fraction of facts that occur exactly once in the training data". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14648)
  - *Difference for all three:* repetition during training, not duplicated evidence at inference time.

### Inferences
- The claim "repetition in context raises acceptance and lowers uncertainty" is already published (Fastowski & Kasneci 2024, Xu et al. 2024, Bouleimen et al. 2026, and Schuster et al. 2026 in KQ2). Paper 1 cannot claim it as new. The open part is the downstream behavioral link (verification and re-observation under cost) in an agent with memory, plus a provenance and independence control.
- The model dependence (GPT-4 is "nearly immune" to plain repetition in Farm, and GPT-5-nano is mildly skeptical in Bouleimen et al.) suggests the duplication effect may be weak in frontier planners. Paper 1 should pre-register heterogeneity across planners and include at least one model where the effect is known to appear.
- The token-level self-reinforcement mechanism (Xu et al. 2022) implies that probability-based confidence measures are partly confounded by plain copying. See also Kook et al. 2026 in KQ3.

### Gaps
- I found no LLM study that measures calibration (ECE, Brier, reliability diagrams) as a function of in-context repetition count. Fastowski & Kasneci report uncertainty shifts only.
- I did not verify Anthropic's "Many-shot jailbreaking" (Anil et al., NeurIPS 2024), which reportedly finds power-law growth of in-context effects with the number of demonstrations. It is not on arXiv, so treat it as unverified.
- Human work on the illusory-truth effect and on "illusion of consensus" or source dependence (e.g., Yousif, Aboody & Keil 2019) is outside this LLM-focused scope and was not checked here.

## Key Question 2: Knowledge-conflict and RAG studies on frequency and majority: do LLMs side with the more frequent evidence, and does anyone control for source dependence (one source duplicated vs. independent sources)?

### Takeaway
Yes. LLMs reliably side with the side that has more evidence: Xie et al. ("LLMs follow the herd"), Jin et al. ("majority rule"), PoisonedRAG (5 injected passages give 90% attack success) and RAMDocs (imbalance hurts). Only one paper explicitly tests one source duplicated vs. different sources: **Schuster, Gautam & Markert (2026)**. They find that verbatim duplication flips source-credibility preferences about as much whether the copies are attributed to one source or to two, while two sources merged into one entry barely move preferences. The models respond to repeated tokens, not to the count of independent sources. No paper links this to confidence calibration or to whether the model verifies.

### Cited Findings
- **[Overlap: HIGH] Whose Facts Win? LLM Source Preferences under Knowledge Conflicts.** Jakob Schuster, Vagrant Gautam, Katja Markert (2026). arXiv preprint (v3, Apr 2026; no venue listed). arXiv:2601.03746. *Method:* synthetic sources (government, newspaper, person, social media) attached to conflicting tables. 13 open-weight LLMs (Qwen2.5 7–72B, OLMo-2 7–32B, Llama-3.x 3–70B, Gemma-3 4–27B). Preference is measured from normalized answer-token probabilities, with bootstrap tests and Holm–Bonferroni correction. *Finding:* LLMs prefer "institutionally-corroborated information" (government or newspaper) over people and social media, but "these source preferences can be reversed by simply repeating information from less credible sources". *Source-dependence control (full text):* there are three conditions against one government table:
  - "Repetition": two identical tables attributed to the **same** social-media source. Shift = 30.04 (absolute change in source preference from the no-repetition baseline, averaged over models).
  - "2-Table Majority": two identical tables attributed to **two different** social-media sources. Shift = 33.90.
  - "1-Table Majority": two sources listed in **one** table header, so nothing is duplicated. Shift = 6.17.

  Unattributed repetition also flips preferences. Only one extra copy (2 copies in total) is tested. A LoRA self-distillation fix (KL penalty on repeated inputs) reduces repetition bias by up to 79.2% while keeping at least 72.5% of the original preferences. *Difference:* static text QA with knowledge conflicts. There is no copy-count sweep beyond 2 and no calibration analysis (the authors do not assess calibration). There is no verification, re-observation or action, no agent memory, no images, and context length is not reported as held fixed (the repetition conditions add a table; my reading of the design, not stated by the authors). **This is the most important prior work for Paper 1's provenance manipulation.** It predicts that same-source labels alone may not remove the effect. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03746); details from [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03746v3)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] Adaptive Chameleon or Stubborn Sloth: Revealing the Behavior of Large Language Models in Knowledge Conflicts.** Jian Xie, Kai Zhang, Jiangjie Chen, Renze Lou, Yu Su (2024). ICLR 2024 (Spotlight). arXiv:2305.13300. *Method:* elicit each model's parametric memory, build coherent counter-memory, and give the model single- or multi-source evidence. Answers are multiple choice with an "Uncertain" option. *Findings:*
  - From the abstract: LLMs accept coherent counter-evidence but show strong confirmation bias.
  - From the full text, Section 4.2: "LLMs follow the herd and choose the side with more evidence". For ChatGPT on PopQA, the memory-answer rate rises from 3.7% (0 of 2 passages support memory) to 99.8% (2 of 2). Confirmation bias also grows with the absolute amount of evidence at a fixed proportion (1/2 vs. 2/4).
  - The models are sensitive to evidence order (more than 30% swings for PaLM2 and Llama2-7B), and confirmation bias is stronger for popular knowledge.

  *Difference:* the multiple pieces of evidence are **distinct passages** (generated memory or counter-memory, Wikipedia), never duplicates. There is no same-source vs. independent-source control and no agent behavior. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13300); Section 4.2 details read via [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.13300)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] Tug-of-War Between Knowledge: Exploring and Resolving Knowledge Conflicts in Retrieval-Augmented Language Models.** Zhuoran Jin, Pengfei Cao, Yubo Chen, et al. (2024). LREC-COLING 2024. arXiv:2402.14409. *Finding:* RALMs "follow the principle of majority rule, leaning towards placing trust in evidence that appears more frequently". They also show confirmation bias, and stronger models show a "Dunning-Kruger effect" (they keep their faulty internal memory). *Setup (full text, Section 5):* LLaMA2-7B only. K ∈ {5, 10, 20} pieces of evidence, with truthful:misleading ratios in {2:0, 2:1, 2:2, 1:2, 0:2}. The metric is recall, and no confidence was measured for this experiment. The paper does not say whether the frequent evidence was duplicated or distinct, and it notes "more external evidence is not necessarily better". *Difference:* one small model, no source-dependence control, no behavior. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14409); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14409v1)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–LOW] Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Conflicting Evidence (RAMDocs / MADAM-RAG).** Han Wang, Archiki Prasad, Elias Stengel-Eskin, Mohit Bansal (2025). COLM 2025. arXiv:2504.13079. *Finding:* RAMDocs mixes ambiguity, misinformation and noise across documents (Llama-3.3-70B gets 32.60 exact match). A multi-agent debate method helps, but "a substantial gap remains especially when increasing the level of imbalance in supporting evidence and misinformation". *Difference:* document-count imbalance in QA. There is no duplication or provenance manipulation, and no confidence or verification measure. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13079)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–LOW] PoisonedRAG: Knowledge Corruption Attacks to Retrieval-Augmented Generation of Large Language Models.** Wei Zou, Runpeng Geng, Binghui Wang, Jinyuan Jia (2025). USENIX Security 2025. arXiv:2402.07867. *Finding:* 90% attack success "when injecting five malicious texts for each target question into a knowledge database with millions of texts". Tested defenses are insufficient. *Difference:* adversarial, optimized passages (not copies of one misperception). The outcome is the answer only. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07867)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] Blinded by Generated Contexts: How Language Models Merge Generated and Retrieved Contexts When Knowledge Conflicts?** Hexiang Tan, Fei Sun, Wanli Yang, et al. (2024). ACL 2024. arXiv:2401.11911. *Finding:* GPT-4, GPT-3.5 and Llama2 strongly favor **LLM-generated** contexts over retrieved ones, "even when they provide incorrect information". The drivers are greater similarity to the question and the fragmentation of retrieved passages. *Difference:* this is a preference by origin, not by count. It matters for Paper 1 because memory entries written by an LLM may be favored over raw observations. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11911)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] ClashEval: Quantifying the tug-of-war between an LLM's internal prior and external evidence.** Kevin Wu, Eric Wu, James Zou (2024). NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks (venue not re-checked). arXiv:2404.10198. *Finding:* LLMs adopt incorrect retrieved content and override a correct prior "over 60% of the time". Less realistic perturbations are adopted less often, and lower prior confidence (token probability) predicts adoption. *Difference:* one passage, no frequency or provenance manipulation. It does tie adoption to prior confidence. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10198)
- **[Overlap: LOW] What Evidence Do Language Models Find Convincing?** Alexander Wan, Eric Wallace, Dan Klein (2024). ACL 2024. arXiv:2402.11782. *Finding:* LLMs weight how relevant a document is to the query and "largely ignor[e] stylistic features that humans find important", such as scientific references or a neutral tone. *Difference:* features of the evidence, not its count or duplication. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11782)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–LOW] How Susceptible are LLMs to Influence in Prompts?** Sotiris Anagnostidis, Jannis Bulian (2024). arXiv preprint (venue not re-checked). arXiv:2408.11865. *Finding:* Llama, Mixtral and Falcon are strongly swayed by another model's prediction plus explanation, "irrespective of the quality of the explanation". Authoritative or confident framing increases the sway, "but the effect is small in size". *Difference:* source-authority labels on a single input. There are no copies and no verification. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11865)
- **Conformity and herding (majority size, peer format, information cascades):**
  - **[Overlap: MEDIUM] Disentangling the Drivers of LLM Social Conformity: An Uncertainty-Moderated Dual-Process Mechanism.** Huixin Zhong, Yanan Liu, Qi Cao, et al. (2025). arXiv:2508.14918. Information-cascade paradigm (from behavioral economics), 9 LLMs, medical, legal and investment scenarios. "Accuracy and confidence consistently ris[e] with stronger evidence". Under low-to-medium uncertainty LLMs underweight all evidence. Under high uncertainty they "overweight public signals (beta > 1.55 vs. private beta = 0.81)". *Difference:* in cascade experiments the public signals are others' choices, which depend on one another. Overweighting them looks like double-counting dependent evidence (my interpretation; the authors frame it as normative-like influence). This is social, not memory. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14918)
  - **[Overlap: MEDIUM–LOW] Do as We Do, Not as You Think: the Conformity of Large Language Models (BenchForm).** Zhiyuan Weng, Guikun Chen, Wenguan Wang (2025). ICLR 2025 (Oral). arXiv:2501.13381. Measures conformity and independence rates under 5 protocols and analyzes the factors "interaction time and majority size". *Difference:* the majority is made of other agents presented as independent. There is no duplication and no verification action. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13381)
  - **[Overlap: MEDIUM–LOW] Herd Behavior: Investigating Peer Influence in LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems.** Young-Min Cho, Sharath Chandra Guntuku, Lyle Ungar (2025). arXiv preprint. arXiv:2505.21588. Conformity depends on the gap between self-confidence and perceived peer confidence, and "the format in which peer information is presented plays a critical role". *Difference:* peer answers, not copied memory. It is relevant as evidence that presentation format (akin to provenance display) changes herding. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21588)
  - **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Many-to-One Adversarial Consensus: Exposing Multi-Agent Collusion Risks in AI-Based Healthcare.** Adeela Bashir, The Anh Han, Zia Ush Shamszaman (2025). arXiv:2512.03097. Colluding assistant agents create "false consensus" (attack success and harmful-recommendation rates up to 100%), and a guideline-checking verifier agent restores 100% accuracy. *Difference:* adversarial agents; verification is imposed by the design rather than chosen by the model. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03097)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM, counterpoint] InteGround: On the Evaluation of Verification and Retrieval Planning in Integrative Grounding.** Cheng Jiayang, Qianqian Zhuang, Haoran Li, et al. (2025). EMNLP 2025 Findings. arXiv:2509.16534. *Finding:* in groundedness verification, "LLMs are robust to redundant evidence" but rationalize with internal knowledge when information is incomplete. *Difference:* "redundant" here appears to mean superfluous extra evidence, not duplicated false evidence (not checked in the full text). It is still a published claim of robustness to redundancy that Paper 1 should address. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16534)
- **[Overlap: LOW] ConflictRAG: Detecting and Resolving Knowledge Conflicts in Retrieval Augmented Generation.** Chenyu Wang, Yueyuan Li, Yingmin Liu, Yang Shu (2026). Submitted to IEEE SMC 2026. arXiv:2605.17301. A pipeline with conflict detection and source-credibility scoring (Entropy-TOPSIS). *Difference:* an engineering mitigation, with no behavioral study of duplication. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17301)

### Inferences
- The claim "LLMs side with the majority or more frequent evidence" is well established (Xie, Jin, Schuster, PoisonedRAG, RAMDocs). The specific claim "LLMs fail to discount same-source duplicates" has one direct precedent: Schuster et al. 2026, with only 2 copies, text tables and answer probabilities. Their finding that duplicated tokens, not distinct sources, drive the shift (30.04 single source vs. 33.90 two sources vs. 6.17 merged) is the closest result to Paper 1's mechanism and must be cited and contrasted.
- Design implication: Schuster's "1-Table Majority" control, where several sources are listed in one entry without duplicated text, is a ready-made way to separate "token duplication" from "claimed number of sources". Paper 1's copies × provenance factorial design could add (a) one entry that lists N sources, and (b) N entries paraphrased, not verbatim, to split lexical repetition from source count.
- No KQ2 paper measures calibration, verification or downstream action, and none involves multimodal grounding (images held fixed). Paper 1's added value lies there.
- Xie et al. found that confirmation bias grows with the absolute amount of evidence at a fixed proportion (1/2 vs. 2/4). That bears directly on Paper 1's goal of holding context length fixed. Absolute evidence volume may matter separately from the copy ratio.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether Jin et al.'s "more frequent" evidence used verbatim duplicates or distinct passages; the paper does not say.
- I did not read the second half of Schuster et al. (characters 100,000 onward). I found no copy-count sweep beyond 2 or context-length control in the part I read.
- I did not run Semantic Scholar citation chains forward from Schuster et al. 2026 or Xie et al. 2024. Very recent citers (after mid-2026) might include an agentic follow-up; this is a residual risk.

## Key Question 3: Correlated errors across LLMs, and self-consistency or ensembles failing because samples are correlated

### Takeaway
Errors are strongly correlated across LLMs: models agree 60% of the time when both are wrong (Kim et al., ICML 2025), and mistakes grow more similar as capability rises (Goel et al. 2025). Errors are also correlated across samples of one model: self-consistency can amplify positional errors (Byerly & Khashabi 2024), and voting accuracy can fall as more calls are added (Chen et al. 2024). Agreement is therefore weaker evidence than independence assumes. All of this concerns aggregating outputs, not duplicated inputs inside one agent's context. A 2026 result (Kook et al.) shows that copying retrieved text into context mechanically inflates token log-probabilities, a direct measurement hazard for Paper 1.

### Cited Findings
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Correlated Errors in Large Language Models.** Elliot Kim, Avi Garg, Kenny Peng, Nikhil Garg (2025). ICML 2025. arXiv:2506.07962. *Method:* over 350 LLMs on two leaderboards and a résumé-screening task. *Finding:* "models agree 60% of the time when both models err". Shared architecture or provider increases correlation, and "larger and more accurate models have highly correlated errors, even with distinct architectures and providers". This affects LLM-as-judge and hiring (algorithmic monoculture). *Difference:* correlation across models on static benchmarks. For Paper 1 it implies that two planner LLMs, or an LLM verifier, are not independent checks. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Great Models Think Alike and this Undermines AI Oversight.** Shashwat Goel, Joschka Strüber, Ilze Amanda Auzina, et al. (2025). Venue not re-checked (believed ICML 2025). arXiv:2502.04313. *Finding:* the CAPA similarity metric shows that LLM judges favor models similar to themselves, and that "model mistakes are becoming more similar with increasing capabilities, pointing to risks from correlated failures". *Difference:* oversight and evaluation, not agent memory. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04313)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Shared Imagination: LLMs Hallucinate Alike.** Yilun Zhou, Caiming Xiong, Silvio Savarese, Chien-Sheng Wu (2024). arXiv preprint (venue not re-checked). arXiv:2407.16604. *Finding:* in "imaginary question answering", models answer each other's made-up questions "with remarkable success", which suggests a shared hallucination space. *Difference:* homogeneity across models, not in-context duplication. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16604)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Are More LLM Calls All You Need? Towards Scaling Laws of Compound Inference Systems.** Lingjiao Chen, Jared Quincy Davis, Boris Hanin, et al. (2024). NeurIPS 2024 (venue not re-checked). arXiv:2403.02419. *Finding:* Vote and Filter-Vote performance "can first increase but then decrease" as LM calls are added. More calls help on easy queries and hurt on hard ones, where the majority answer is systematically wrong. *Difference:* aggregating outputs, not inputs. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02419)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Self-Consistency Falls Short! The Adverse Effects of Positional Bias on Long-Context Problems.** Adam Byerly, Daniel Khashabi (2024). arXiv preprint. arXiv:2411.01101. *Finding:* self-consistency "not only fails to improve but actively degrades performance on long-context tasks" because persistent position bias makes samples err together. "Long-context SC amplifies positional errors". *Difference:* correlation induced by context structure across samples. It is the closest "correlated samples" result to context effects, but it has no duplication or provenance. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01101)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM, measurement hazard] Beyond Confidence: Test-Time Scaling for Multi-Turn Search Agents via Retrieval Grounding.** Hyunho Kook, Junhyuk So, Tianyu Fu, Haizhong Zheng, Beidi Chen (2026). EMNLP 2026 Findings. arXiv:2608.24024. *Finding:* "copy inflation". When retrieved documents are appended to an agent's context, "tokens copied from those documents receive systematically inflated log probabilities". This flattens confidence scores and breaks confidence-weighted voting. Their fix, retrieval-grounded voting, is computed outside the context. *Difference:* search agents and voting. For Paper 1, it means copies in memory will raise log-prob confidence mechanically, so calibration should not rest on token probabilities alone. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24024)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–LOW] Diverse Evidence, Better Forecasts: Multi-Agent Deliberation Under Information Asymmetry (InfoDelphi).** Yuante Li, Yicheng Tao, Kate Zhang, et al. (2026). arXiv:2607.01661. *Finding:* "When all agents are given identical evidence, deliberation collapses into herding". Splitting evidence into public and private subsets provably reduces inter-agent error correlation, improving Brier score by 12–18% on PolyGym. *Difference:* shared vs. split evidence across agents. It is analogous to a shared memory feeding all modules the same error, but it does not cover verification or embodiment. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01661)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Minority Sentinel: When to Overturn Majority Voting in Multi-Agent LLM Debates.** Chuan He, Zebin Chen, Zhengyi Yang, et al. (2026). AgentSearch Workshop @ SIGIR 2026. arXiv:2606.29270. *Finding:* the Condorcet independence assumption fails because LLM errors are "strongly correlated". In about 1 in 4 divergent cases the minority is right, and a LightGBM meta-classifier overturns the majority with 81.2% flip precision. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29270)
- **[Overlap: LOW] Beyond Majority Voting: LLM Aggregation by Leveraging Higher-Order Information.** Rui Ai, Yuqi Pan, David Simchi-Levi, Milind Tambe, Haifeng Xu (2026). ICML 2026. arXiv:2510.01499. Optimal Weight and Inverse Surprising Popularity aggregation account for "latent heterogeneity and correlation across models". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01499)
- **[Overlap: LOW] Examining Independence in Ensemble Sentiment Analysis: A Study on the Limits of LLMs Using the Condorcet Jury Theorem.** Baptiste Lefort, Eric Benhamou, et al. (2024). arXiv:2409.00094. Majority votes across models including GPT-4 give "only marginal improvements", "suggesting a lack of independence among them". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00094)
- **[Overlap: LOW] Debate or Vote: Which Yields Better Decisions in Multi-Agent Large Language Models?** Hyeong Kyu Choi, Xiaojin Zhu, Sharon Li (2025). NeurIPS 2025 (Spotlight). arXiv:2508.17536. Majority voting explains most gains from multi-agent debate. Self-recognition correlates linearly with self-preference. *Difference:* evaluation bias. It loosely supports the risk that an LLM over-trusts memory text written by the same LLM (see Tan et al. 2024 in KQ2). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)

### Inferences
- Paper 1's mechanism (one LLM call writes the same misperception into several stores) is a within-agent case of correlated errors. Several "sources" that share one generator are like ensemble members with correlation close to 1. No paper in this cluster studies that within-agent, input-side case. They all study output aggregation across samples or models.
- Kim et al. and Goel et al. warn that "at least two planner LLMs" should not be framed as independent replications of an effect. Their errors and susceptibilities may themselves be correlated. Families from different providers (and the agreement-when-both-err statistic) help argue generality.
- Kook et al.'s copy inflation means a log-prob confidence will rise with copy count even if the model's beliefs do not change. Paper 1 should use verbalized confidence, behavioral confidence (betting or abstention choices) and choice-based verification, or apply a no-copy lexical-overlap control.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the claim, which I recall from memory, that semantic entropy (Farquhar et al., Nature 2024) cannot detect consistent, systematic errors; I did not check it.
- "Talk Isn't Always Cheap: Understanding Failure Modes in Multi-Agent Debate" (2025) is a likely relevant paper on debate conformity, but a title search on arXiv did not find it, so it is unverified and omitted.

## Key Question 4: Hallucination snowballing and self-reinforcing errors when a model conditions on its own earlier outputs (including memory)

### Takeaway
This is well established. Models over-commit to their own earlier errors even when they could detect those errors in isolation (snowballing, self-conditioning, the self-correction blind spot). In vision-language models, earlier hallucinated text overrides visual evidence that is still available (MMHalSnowball, MM-Snowball, ViF). In agents with memory, stored errors propagate to later tasks (experience-following, laundering, flooding). None of these varies the number of copies or the claimed provenance of the propagated error. None measures calibration or the choice to verify or re-observe under cost. One (Tsui 2026) does manipulate attribution (self vs. user) with identical content, which is methodologically closest to Paper 1's provenance-label intervention.

### Cited Findings
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] How Language Model Hallucinations Can Snowball.** Muru Zhang, Ofir Press, William Merrill, Alisa Liu, Noah A. Smith (2023/2024). ICML 2024 (venue not re-checked). arXiv:2305.13534. *Finding:* on 3 QA datasets, ChatGPT and GPT-4 justify early wrong answers with false claims that they "can separately recognize as incorrect". They identify 67% and 87% of their own mistakes, respectively, when these are shown separately. "An LM over-commits to early mistakes". *Difference:* a single error in the model's own generation, not duplicated copies. There is no verification action. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13534)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] The Illusion of Diminishing Returns: Measuring Long Horizon Execution in LLMs.** Akshit Sinha, Arvindh Arun, Shashwat Goel, Steffen Staab, Jonas Geiping (2026). ICLR 2026. arXiv:2509.09677. *Finding:* a "self-conditioning effect": "models become more likely to make mistakes when the context contains their errors from prior turns". Scaling model size does not reduce it, but "thinking mitigates self-conditioning". *Difference:* errors in execution traces, not duplicated evidence. Still, it shows that the content of earlier errors in context causally raises later error rates, and that thinking or reasoning modes may weaken the effect (a moderator Paper 1 should log). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09677)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–HIGH for provenance method] Self-Correction Bench: Uncovering and Addressing the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Large Language Models.** Ken Tsui (2025/2026). COLM 2026. arXiv:2507.02778. *Method:* the same error is injected as either user-attributed or model-attributed, "keeping all other context identical". *Finding:* across 14 open non-reasoning models there is a "64.5% Self-Correction Blind Spot": models fix the error when it is attributed to the user but not when it is attributed to themselves. Appending "Wait" reduces the blind spot by 89.3%, and training on 5,306 error-correction traces reduces it by 76.0%. A conversational-role direction in representation space causally gates correction. *Difference:* attribution is self vs. user, not one source vs. many. The outcome is correction, not a costly verification action. It is the strongest precedent that a provenance label alone, with content held fixed, changes error handling. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02778)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–HIGH] Investigating and Mitigating the Multimodal Hallucination Snowballing in Large Vision-Language Models (MMHalSnowball).** Weihong Zhong, Xiaocheng Feng, Liang Zhao, et al. (2024). ACL 2024. arXiv:2407.00569. *Finding:* LVLMs answer visual questions inside a conversation that contains an earlier hallucination. Open-source LVLMs drop at least 31%, "prone to accept the generated hallucinations ... even though the ground visual information exists". Residual Visual Decoding mitigates more than 24%. *Difference:* one hallucination in dialogue history, not copies across memory stores. There is no verification choice. It is the strongest evidence that text history can override an image that stays fixed (as Paper 1 holds images fixed). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00569)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] MM-Snowball: Evaluating and Mitigating Hallucination Snowballing in Multimodal Multi-Turn Dialogue.** Yue Jiang, Xue Jiang, Lihua Zhang, et al. (2026). ICML 2026. arXiv:2606.00622. *Finding:* initial errors amplify across turns as models "progressively neglect visual grounding in favor of over-relying on polluted textual history". Single-turn mitigations fail. CAVR re-anchors the model to visual facts. *Difference:* dialogue, with no duplication or verification behavior. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00622)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] Visual Multi-Agent System: Mitigating Hallucination Snowballing via Visual Flow (ViF).** Xinlei Yu, Chengming Xu, Guibin Zhang, et al. (2025). arXiv:2509.21789 (venue not re-checked). *Finding:* in VLM multi-agent systems, hallucinations are "seeded in a single agent and amplified by following ones due to the over-reliance on textual flow to relay visual information". Visual attention shrinks in deeper agent turns. *Difference:* a relay between agents, not shared memory copies. There is no verification decision. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21789)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–HIGH] How Memory Management Impacts LLM Agents: An Empirical Study of Experience-Following Behavior.** Zidi Xiong, Yuping Lin, Wenya Xie, et al. (2025). arXiv:2505.16067. *Finding:* an "experience-following property": high similarity between a task and a retrieved memory record leads to highly similar outputs. This causes "error propagation, where inaccuracies in past experiences compound and degrade future performance", and "misaligned experience replay". *Difference:* memory addition and deletion policies across tasks. There are no copies across stores, no provenance labels, and no calibration or verification. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16067)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] Flooding Spread of Manipulated Knowledge in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Communities.** Tianjie Ju, Yiting Wang, Xinbei Ma, et al. (2026). Science China Information Sciences 69, 172103. arXiv:2407.07791. *Finding:* injected counterfactual or toxic knowledge spreads through agent communication, and the manipulations "persist through popular retrieval-augmented generation frameworks, where several benign agents store and retrieve manipulated chat histories". *Difference:* adversarial and multi-agent. There is no single-misperception duplication and no verification measurement. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07791)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] Agent Memory Is a Surface for Endogenous Authorization Laundering (EAL-Bench).** Tommaso Cerruti, Mika Okamoto, Ansel Kaplan Erol (2026). arXiv:2609.01836. *Finding:* LLM memory writers create false authority for up to 50.2% of unauthorized requests. "Once false authority is present, executors act on it in 98.6% of trials". Requiring stored permissions to be "backed by valid source events" (provenance) reduces laundering but rejects more legitimate actions. *Difference:* the authorization domain. It is the clearest case of "an error written into memory by an LLM loses its provenance, then is acted on without checking", but it has no copy-count manipulation and no perception. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01836)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] From Spark to Fire: Modeling and Mitigating Error Cascades in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Collaboration.** Yizhe Xie, Congcong Zhu, Xinyue Zhang, et al. (2026). arXiv:2603.04474. *Finding:* minor errors "solidify into system-level false consensus through iteration". There are three vulnerability classes (cascade amplification, topological sensitivity, "consensus inertia"), and one atomic error seed can cause widespread failure. A genealogy-graph (lineage) governance layer prevents final infection in at least 89% of runs. *Difference:* the multi-agent message graph. The lineage tracking it uses is close to Paper 1's provenance idea but serves as a mitigation; it does not test calibration or verification behavior. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04474)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–LOW] The Hallucination Snowball: Modeling Error Propagation as State Transitions in Multi-Agent LLM Pipelines.** Prabhjot Singh, Bhushan Pawar (2026). FAGEN Workshop @ ICML 2026. arXiv:2608.14588. *Finding:* injected hallucinations change form across stages (raw fact, then a derived value, then narrative text, until the error is no longer visible). The chance that an error gets past each stage boundary is 24.6%, 48.3% and 89.3%. GPT-4o detection falls from 72.0% at Stage 1 to 50.9% at Stage 4. Verification gates at each boundary cut survival from 58.4% to 16.2%, while checking only at the end gains just 2.3 pp. *Difference:* a financial pipeline where the verification schedule is fixed, not chosen by the agent. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14588)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation.** Philippe Laban, Hiroaki Hayashi, Yingbo Zhou, Jennifer Neville (2025). arXiv:2505.06120. Performance drops 39% on average in multi-turn vs. single-turn settings. Models make early assumptions and "overly rely" on them: "when LLMs take a wrong turn ... they get lost and do not recover". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Encouraging Divergent Thinking in Large Language Models through Multi-Agent Debate.** Tian Liang, Zhiwei He, Wenxiang Jiao, et al. (2024). EMNLP 2024. arXiv:2305.19118. "Degeneration-of-Thought": "once the LLM has established confidence in its solutions, it is unable to generate novel thoughts later through reflection even if its initial stance is incorrect". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19118)
- **[Overlap: LOW] Are You Sure? Challenging LLMs Leads to Performance Drops in The FlipFlop Experiment.** Philippe Laban, Lidiya Murakhovs'ka, Caiming Xiong, Chien-Sheng Wu (2023). arXiv:2311.08596. Models flip 46% of answers when challenged, and accuracy drops 17% on average. This is the opposite failure (too little persistence) and serves as a useful contrast. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08596)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Memory-safety benchmarks and governance:**
  - MemEvoBench (Weiwei Xie et al., 2026, arXiv:2604.15774) defines "memory misevolution" as "gradual behavioral drift resulting from repeated exposure to misleading information" in mixed benign and misleading memory pools. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15774)
  - MemGuard (Haoyu Wang et al., 2026, arXiv:2608.21867) names "memory drift" from "duplicate, stale, and conflicting records" and stores verifier signals as lifecycle metadata. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21867)
  - GraphWake (Haoran Bu et al., 2026, arXiv:2608.17665) shows a "memory-mediated polarization cascade" across agent communities. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17665)
  - *Difference for all three:* engineering benchmarks or defenses, with no causal copy-count or provenance manipulation and no calibration.

### Inferences
- Paper 1's "errors persist and spread to progress judgments and next actions" rests on established snowballing and self-conditioning effects. The new part is (a) duplication across stores as the amplifier, (b) whether provenance labels reduce it, and (c) the mediating role of fewer verification choices.
- Tsui 2026 suggests an extra provenance condition: copies labeled as written by the planner or memory module vs. by the perception module vs. by independent observations. Self-attribution may suppress correction separately from the number of copies.
- MMHalSnowball, MM-Snowball and ViF show that VLMs neglect visual tokens when textual history is polluted. Holding images fixed while varying copies is therefore a clean test that has not yet been run in embodied memory.

### Gaps
- I found no embodied-agent study (e.g., BEHAVIOR-1K, ALFRED, Habitat) of snowballing caused by memory duplication. Embodied-memory work may be covered by another researcher's notes.
- I did not check whether Xiong et al. 2025 report any verification-like behavior (for example, re-checking retrieved experiences).

## Key Question 5: Does any paper measure how duplicated context changes an LLM agent's decision to verify, search, call a tool or ask for help?

### Takeaway
I found no paper that manipulates duplicated or same-source copies and measures the agent's own choice to verify, re-observe, search, call a tool or ask for help, let alone across a sweep of verification costs. The nearest neighbors are:
- **ECMR (Liu et al., Oct 2026):** a controller that treats "repeated support" mistaken for "independent confirmation" as "semantic false consensus" and triggers verification based on support diversity and independence.
- **ToxicBench (Tao & Yin, Sep 2026):** repeated poisoning defeats checking.
- **Work showing that context makes agents stop looking or become overconfident:** semantic layers, evidence tools, insufficient context.

This makes the central behavioral claim of Paper 1 apparently open as of 2026-10-21, within the limits of these searches.

### Cited Findings
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–HIGH, closest concept] ECMR: Evidence-Aware Deliberation Control for Multi-Agent LLM Reasoning.** Yuan Liu, Haoyue Peng, Fanliang Hu, Lei Huang, Wenli Cai (2026, posted 2026-10-13). arXiv:2610.17869. *Method:* a budgeted controller over frozen role-conditioned LLMs. At each step it summarizes "answer concentration, support diversity and independence, contradictions, role behavior, verification history, and remaining budget", then chooses to propose, challenge, verify, arbitrate, diversify or stop. *Finding:* a fixed deliberation protocol "may mistake repeated support for independent confirmation", which they call "semantic false consensus: a confident group answer supported by narrow, duplicated, or correlated rationales". On 9,000 MedQA, CaseHOLD and FinQA records, false-consensus rate falls from 40.7% to 17.0% and accuracy rises from 62.1% to 84.9%. *Difference:* multi-agent text QA. Verification is decided by an external controller, not measured as the LLM's own propensity. There is no causal copy-count or provenance intervention on a planner (from the abstract only), no embodiment, no memory stores and no images. **This is the paper most likely to be raised by reviewers as prior art for "duplicated support should trigger verification".** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17869)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] When Tools Silently Lie: Evaluating and Mitigating Blind Compliance in Tool-Augmented Data Agents (ToxicBench).** Zifu Tao, Changqing Yin (2026). arXiv:2609.37153. *Method:* clean vs. poisoned tool observations over fixed source data, measuring both checking and adoption. *Finding:* poisoning lowers task success by 26–39 pp. "Ordinary retries help under one-shot poisoning, whereas repeated poisoning reveals wrong-answer adoption after checking". *Difference:* data agents and tool outputs. Repetition here means the check returns the same wrong value (correlated verification), not duplicated memory. It is directly relevant to Paper 1's case where re-observation by the same faulty perceiver reproduces the error. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37153)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM] The Confidence Dichotomy: Analyzing and Mitigating Miscalibration in Tool-Use Agents.** Weihao Xuan, Qingcheng Zeng, Heli Qi, et al. (2026). arXiv:2601.07264. *Finding:* "evidence tools (e.g., web search) systematically induce severe overconfidence due to inherent noise in retrieved information, while verification tools (e.g., code interpreters) ... mitigate miscalibration". An RL method jointly optimizes accuracy and calibration. *Difference:* the type of tool, not duplication. It does show that retrieved evidence inflates verbalized confidence in agents. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07264)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–LOW] Same Context, Different Agents: A Behavioral Study of Semantic Layers, Orchestration and Compaction in Data Agents.** Zhivar Sourati, Marianne Menglin Liu, Zheng Wang, et al. For investigators, "the semantic layer stops them from looking, orchestration makes them overconfident, costing 5 percentage points, and compaction makes them repeat steps". *Difference:* context-engineering techniques, not duplicated evidence. It does show that adding description-like context to a prompt can suppress inspection, which is the behavior Paper 1 predicts. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20959)
- **[Overlap: MEDIUM–LOW] Sufficient Context: A New Lens on Retrieval Augmented Generation Systems.** Hailey Joren, Jianyi Zhang, Chun-Sung Ferng, et al. (2025). ICLR 2025 (venue not re-checked). arXiv:2411.06037. *Finding:* larger models (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5) "often output incorrect answers instead of abstaining when the context is not [sufficient]". Selective generation guided by sufficient context improves the fraction of correct answers among responses by 2–10%. *Difference:* the outcome is abstention, not verification, and there is no duplication. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] Loud Failures, Quiet Failures: Fault Detection and Recovery in Tool-Using Language Model Agents.** Obada Kraishan (2026). arXiv:2610.10062. *Finding:* agents flag a problem in 91.3% of trials for explicit errors but only 58.8% for "a plausible wrong value". Reasoning models notice less (−9.3 points). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10062)
- **[Overlap: LOW–MEDIUM] When the harness decides success: state that cannot be re-observed.** Raimondas Lencevicius (2026, pre-registered). arXiv:2610.26632. *Finding:* truncating context costs nothing when a needed fact "can be looked up again (it looks the fact up instead)". Success drops to chance when the fact cannot be re-observed, a 92.5 pp difference. *Difference:* availability of re-observation, not duplication. EMNLP 2025. arXiv:2505.17281. *Finding:* over-search and under-search are common (27.7% of one model's search steps were avoidable) and are linked to uncertainty about the model's knowledge boundary. *Difference:* no manipulation of context duplication. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17281)

Its causal-intervention diagnosis is a methodological analogue. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03304)

  - From Agent Traces to Trust (Yiqi Wang et al., 2026, arXiv:2606.04990) is a survey of execution provenance. It notes gaps in "memory and multi-agent lineage" and finds that no catalogued benchmark evaluates the full provenance chain. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990)
  - Selection Integrity for LLM Graph Memory (Zeming Fei et al., 2026, arXiv:2606.12290) shows that provenance checks on retrieved records are "blind by construction" to structural writes that change which facts are selected. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12290)

### Inferences
- Combining KQ1–KQ5: the parts already published are (i) repetition and majority changing answers, (ii) repetition lowering uncertainty, (iii) same-source duplication not being discounted (2 copies, text QA), (iv) own errors snowballing, including in VLMs, and (v) memory errors propagating. I found no paper that shows (vi) duplication or same-source copies in memory reducing the agent's own verification or re-observation choices, with calibration measured and a verification-cost sweep, in embodied tasks. Paper 1's novelty should be stated around (vi), plus the factorial control of copies × provenance with images and context length held fixed.
- Expect reviewers to cite ECMR (duplicated support as false consensus; verification triggered by independence), Schuster et al. (repetition beats provenance), and Fastowski & Kasneci (repetition lowers uncertainty). Paper 1 should cite all three and explain how a causal, single-planner, embodied, behavioral-verification study differs.
- ToxicBench's "adoption after checking" under repeated poisoning implies Paper 1 must separate two failures: not verifying, and verifying with a correlated check (re-observing with the same faulty perceiver). The verification-cost sweep should log both whether the agent chose to verify and whether verification gave independent evidence.

### Gaps
- I did not run full-text searches inside OpenReview submissions (ICLR 2027 or NeurIPS 2026 under review). Unpublished concurrent work on duplication-induced under-verification cannot be ruled out.
- ECMR was checked from its abstract only. Whether it reports a controlled manipulation of duplicated rationales and the LLMs' own verification rates is not verified.
- I found no study of verification-cost sweeps (a value-of-information curve) for LLM agents with manipulated evidence redundancy.

### Inferences
- Suggested novelty framing for Paper 1, based on the matrix: "Prior work shows that LLMs side with repeated or majority evidence (Xie 2024; Jin 2024), do not discount same-source repetition in text QA (Schuster 2026), become less uncertain under repetition (Fastowski 2024), and snowball their own errors (Zhang 2023; Zhong 2024; Sinha 2026). Multi-agent controllers now treat duplicated support as false consensus (ECMR 2026). No work causally tests whether one-source duplication across an embodied agent's memory stores lowers the planner's own costly verification, with calibration and error persistence measured and images and context length held fixed."
- The most likely rebuttal risk is ECMR (posted 8 days before this check) and any follow-up applying it to agents. Re-check arXiv for ECMR citers before submission.
